Wiring

Reference diagrams for using betacrawler with real hardware. This page grows over time as new boards and setups get documented.

Skid steer with brushless motors

A WeAct Black Pill — STM32F411CE or F401CE, the two are pin-compatible — wired for the full skid-steer brushless stack: LiPo into a power distribution board, PDB into both ESCs and the board's 5V rail, ESCs out to the brushless motors, and a CRSF receiver on the serial port. Pin names come straight from the board headers and are identical on both chips — this is a schematic wiring map, not the board's physical silkscreen layout, so match connections by label, not by position.

Skid steer with brushed motors

The same board wired for a DRV8833-class dual H-bridge module driving two brushed DC gearmotors instead of brushless ESCs — pick brushed for Type on the Configuration page, no reflash needed. motor0/motor1 use the same PA6/PB8 pins either way, plus a second pin each (PA7/PB9) for the H-bridge's other input. The battery pack feeds the H-bridge module directly — the board's own power still comes from USB or the receiver, exactly like an ESC build; a brushed build does not power the board from the drive-motor pack.

Gold = the H-bridge's control signals from the board (PA6/PA7 to motor0, PB8/PB9 to motor1). Navy = the two motor leads out of each H-bridge channel — either order runs the motor, swapping a pair reverses it, or set that motor's Invert in the app instead. Teal = the power chain: the pack feeds the H-bridge's VM/GND directly, with no board or PDB in that path. Violet = the CRSF link, unchanged from the ESC build. The module's SLEEP pin must be jumpered to VCC to run at all — some breakouts truncate that pin's silkscreen label to EEP — and a common ground between the pack, the module, and the board is required, not optional.

Car with a brushless motor

One driven motor plus a steering servo, instead of two driven sides — set Drive Mode to car on the Configuration page, no reflash needed. motor0 keeps PA6 and takes the mixer's throttle output; the servo goes on PB10 and takes its steer output. motor1 is unused on a single-motor car — leave its mode off. The ESC must treat centre-stick as stop, which a surface (car/boat) ESC already does.

Green = the ESC's control signal from PA6. Amber = the steering servo on PB10. Teal = the pack and the ESC's BEC, which powers the board, the receiver and the servo. Navy = the three motor phases. Purple = the CRSF receiver.

Car with a brushed motor

A DRV8833-class H-bridge driving the car's brushed motor, plus a steering servo on PB10 — set Drive Mode to car and Type to brushed on the Configuration page, no reflash needed. Both motor outputs carry throttle in car mode, so a single motor works on either pair of pins and a two-wheel-drive car works by wiring both. Unlike a brushless car there is no ESC BEC to borrow, so a separate 5 V BEC off the pack feeds the board, the receiver and the servo.

Gold = the H-bridge's control signals from the board. Navy = the motor leads out of each H-bridge channel. Orange = the steering servo: only its signal comes from the board, on PB10. Teal = the power chain — the pack feeds the H-bridge's VM directly and the BEC alongside it, and the BEC's 5V rail is what the board, the receiver and the servo all run from. Violet = the CRSF link, unchanged from every other build. A common ground between the pack, the module, the BEC and the board is required, not optional.

Add-on: battery sense

Optional on every build — the vehicle drives without it. A resistor divider scales pack voltage into the ADC's 0–3.3V range and lands on PA1, the one pin no base build claims. Tap raw pack voltage: a PDB's VCC pad on a build that has a PDB, or the pack's own positive lead on one that does not. Never a regulated BEC output — a regulator holds steady as the pack drains, so the reading would look healthy right up until the vehicle stops.

Three leads, one free pin, and nothing about your base build changes. Set vbat.source to adc (already the default), then calibrate: read the pack with a multimeter, enter that voltage on the Configuration page, and the scale adjusts so the board agrees.

Add-on: battery sense — divider circuit

Optional — the vehicle drives without it. Fit it if you want pack voltage on the transmitter and in the Configurator. This is the component-level circuit behind the sense card above. A LiPo pack is far above the 3.3V the ADC can read, so a resistor divider scales it down. Only the three terminals are shown here — where they land on the board is in the wiring map above. 47k/4k7 divides by exactly 11: a 4S reads 1.53V and a 6S 2.29V, both comfortably inside range.

Teal = the power chain: the LiPo feeds the PDB, the PDB feeds both ESCs and the board's 5V rail (which in turn powers the receiver). Navy = the three motor leads out of each ESC. Green = the ESC signal leads, the only motor wiring the board itself touches. Violet = the CRSF link, both directions: the receiver's TX carries stick positions in on PA3, and PA2 carries telemetry back out to the handset. Motor 0 drives the left side and Motor 1 the right, both fed by the drive mixer rather than by a receiver channel directly.
